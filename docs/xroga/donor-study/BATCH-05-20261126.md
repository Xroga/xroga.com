## What the tools can do now (summary)

- `media`: info, audio (loudness, peak, silences, a level line), highlights (loud moments as ranges), beats, beatcuts, scenes, frames (with `grid`), look (measurements and the grade that fixes a picture), cover.
- `transcribe`: lines, words, fillers, pauses (padded cut ranges), srt, vtt, outline.
- `compose_video`: spec with clips, layers, 46 transitions, keyframes with 40 easing curves and springs, grade on pictures and videos, adjustment layers, masks and shadows, speed ranges and curves, zooms, text looks, stagger, subtitles from a file with an offset, caption styles, sound treatment, ducking, brand and templates; preview with a grid; `help` by topic; output MP4, WebM or an animated GIF.
- `convert_media`: formats mp4, mov, webm, mkv, gif, ogg, wav, m4a, aac, with trim, resize, crop, rotate, codec and quality.
- `record_screen`, `webmcp`, `chapters`.

## Test evidence

- Unit: the whole extension suite passes (see the end of this file for the final count).
- Chromium, real extension, real internet (no local server), each check measured from pixels or sound, not by eye alone:
  - `S5-media`, `S5-transcribe-words`, `S5-compose`, `S5-compose-editly`, `S5-compose-cutline`, `S5-convert`, `S5-record-screen`, `S5-webmcp` (first pass, still passing).
  - `S5-compose-grade`: a real 17-point LUT (a .cube file from a public MIT repository) changes the colour of a Sintel frame (difference 19); an adjustment layer in black and white leaves no colour (spread 0); auto balance brightens a dark frame (mean 18,13,8 to 41,27,11); a round mask keeps the corners of its box red while the middle shows the film; a zoom on cell C1 changes the frame (difference 110); a speed range gives 8 s from 6 s with 2 s at half speed; speech treated with noise reduction, voice equaliser, compressor and reverb is 7.5 LU louder than the plain one; the grid is drawn on a sheet; `media look` returns measurements for real frames.
  - `S5-compose-more`: the real Sintel SRT is read (26 cues, the first at 107.25 s), written back and read again identically; its captions show on a clip (a region of the frame changes by 36); a typewriter title types in; staggered titles come in one after another; a hero-moment speed curve gives exactly the length worked out (15.6 s); a spring easing and three new transitions render with the expected length; `media highlights` finds four moments in the real trailer; two animated GIFs (from the trailer, 24 pictures at 320 wide; from a composition, 12 pictures) are walked block by block and the first picture is decoded by the browser itself.

## Not done, said plainly

- The noise reduction is spectral subtraction with a profile learned from the quietest stretch; a file with no quiet stretch, or with noise that is part of the wanted sound, will not come out clean. The pixel passes run on the CPU (a 1280×720 clip takes a few times its length to render).
- A GIF is 256 colours with ordered dithering, at most 400 pictures and about 70 MB of pictures in memory at once; the rate is lowered, and said, when that would be passed.
- A .cube LUT must be a 3D one; a 1D LUT is refused. A LUT at an address that needs a sign-in is not reachable.
- The grade of a video is applied to each frame as it is drawn, so a long, large, graded video is slow; `fast` halves the size.
- Auto balance measures the picture once (the first frame that is not black) and keeps that grade for the layer.
- Hint retrieval by embeddings (AgentLab) and word-edge snapping of cuts (Panoptik) are not wired. Stabilisation, upscaling, frame interpolation, motion tracking, multicam, shape masks and particle effects (openreel) are not built.
- Only https addresses (or `recording:last`), 3 minutes, 60 clips, 1920×1080. A file larger than 150 MB is refused. Decoding depends on the browser's codecs.
- ffmpeg.wasm is not vendored (GPL), so a conversion that needs a codec the browser cannot encode is not possible.
