Harbor Tides Return to the Old Quay

By Mira Okonkwo

The old quay reopened on Thursday after eleven weeks of dredging, three weeks ahead of the schedule that the harbour board published in September. Officials said the early finish saved the town roughly 240,000 euros, because the contractor did not need the second barge that had been reserved for December.

What changed

The channel is now 4.2 metres deep at low tide, up from 2.8 metres. Boats of up to 18 metres can berth along the east wall, and the fuel pontoon was moved to the north end so that it no longer blocks the fairway. A full account is in the harbour board report.

  • East wall: 14 berths for boats up to 18 metres
  • North pontoon: fuel and pump-out
  • West steps: reserved for the ferry until March

Local sailors had waited since August for the work to finish. Many had moved their boats to the marina at Westholm, a forty minute drive away, and said that the cost of the extra fuel and the lost weekends had been higher than the berthing fee itself. The sailing club chair, Ana Petrovic, said that members would return in groups over the next fortnight so that the east wall would not be overwhelmed on the first weekend.

The dredging contractor, Brightwater Marine, removed about 61,000 cubic metres of silt and disposed of it at the licensed site beyond the outer breakwater. An independent survey on Tuesday confirmed that the new depth is consistent along the whole length of the channel, and the survey report notes only a small shoal near the fuel pontoon that will be cleared in January during routine maintenance.

Business owners along the quay were cautiously optimistic. The owner of the harbour cafe said that bookings for the December weekends were already ahead of last year, while a chandler said that he expected to restock winter supplies earlier than planned because boat owners were returning sooner than expected. The council said it would review the parking arrangements on Mill Street after the first month of operation.

The harbour master, Tomas Reyes, said the board would publish the booking rules on 1 December. "We expect the east wall to be full by the second weekend," he said. Anyone with questions can write to harbour@gazette.example or call +44 20 7946 0958.

The town council meets on Monday to approve the revised fee table, and a public viewing of the dredging records is open every weekday until the end of the month at the council offices on Mill Street, where the survey drawings and the contractor invoices can be inspected.
